The Super-Mini™ is a completely redesigned Prusa Mini. Every part of the mechanical design, other than the actuators, was reconsidered. This design started with a simple assumption; dynamic elastic displacement scales with moving mass and acceleration, and inversely with structural stiffness. The Super Mini was designed to minimize $m_{\text{moving}}/k_{\text{structure}}$, reducing nozzle-to-bed deflection during high-acceleration motion. The theory is this allows the same firmware to run without additional ghosting while scaled to higher accelerations.

$$
\Delta x_{\text{nozzle-bed}} \approx \frac{m_{\text{moving}}\,a}{k_{\text{structure}}}
$$

To achieve this, mass was added to the base of the printer while the other parts of the structure were made more rigid. The result is a base that weights 2kg due to tungsten inserts in the plastic ends of the y-axis and 500g of steel lab weights. The x-axis is given a third bar to reduce x-axis jitter in the y-plane due to vibration. Furthermore, the stock 30 x 30 riser for the z-axis is replaced with a 30 x 60 channel affixed to a solid aluminum foot plate (machine work by [xometry](https://www.xometry.com/)) that extends across the two 30 x 30 channels of the y-axis reducing any sagging or jitter of the x-axis in the z-plane.

The print head borrows some elements from the Prusa MK3 head and the E3D Revo Hot End adapter for the Mini+ but rotates the Mini's cooling fan to allow for the third rod of the Super Mini's x-axis gantry. The additional weight of adding the third, carbon-fiber rod is made up by using hollow core rods for the first two.

The build makes extensive use of carbon-fiber infused Polycarbonate (the dark gray material) along with the Prusa-standard PETG (orange). Using PC/CF – which allows for less material for the same strength – for the print head and the far end of the x-axis gantry of the x-axis further reduced x-axis weight to ensure fast retractions even with the three-rod gantry.

Finally, the Prusa extruder design is replaced with an E3D Hemera with Bowden tube adapter.

I fully acknowledge this is a somewhat ridiculous design in being extensively over-engineered for such a small, inexpensive printer. The work was more about learning 3D printing for the first time and generally having fun with mechanical design and Fusion 360. The result is a genuinely good printer, however. It has become my goto device for PETG or PLA plastic parts and has proven more reliable than a new PRUSA MK4S I recently purchased.

<Figure
  src={renderCableSnake}

  caption="The cable snake — a drag chain to keep wiring from getting tangled in the y-axis."

A note about the y-axis cable chain for anyone else that owns a PRUSA mini; this is not cosmetic. I'd already lost a print to the stock cable bundle getting tangled up and pushing the magnetic print sheet out of alignment when I decided to add this to the Super Mini's design. Yes, it does looks sort of cool, but it's functional and a recommended upgrade to your printer if you can find a version that works with the stock PRUSA design. The Super Mini takes this a step further and takes the wiring directly into the buddy enclosure through a tunnel in the rear y-axis end piece.
